# Tash Mashup — Business Plan

**San Diego, CA · Plan date: November 2026 · Launch: January 2027**
Companion model: [`Tash_Mashup_Financial_Model.xlsx`](./Tash_Mashup_Financial_Model.xlsx). Every number below comes from that workbook, so if you change an input there, this plan's figures change too.

**Design choices this plan follows:** rotating experiences · flexible venues · side-business ambition · flexible, tiered ticket pricing · intimate dinner-party setting · **never in our own home**.

---

## 1. Executive summary

Tash Mashup is a rotating, ticketed supper club. Each dinner seats **10–12 guests at one communal table** in a space that feels like a home. The menu and theme change every time, and some dinners are collaborations with San Diego chefs, artists, wine shops and creators. Guests buy tickets in advance, so we hold no inventory risk. There is no permanent restaurant and, at first, no lease.

The product is the evening, not just the food: a beautifully set table, a story behind the menu, the hosts at the table, and other people worth meeting.

**What the model says (base case, 10 paid guests per event):**

| | Founding $145 | Signature $175 | Premium $200 | Collaboration $225 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cash contribution per event | $217 | $413 | $560 | $618 |
| What we earn per owner-hour | $13.50 | $25.80 | $31.10 | $44.10 |
| Seats to break even on cash | 8 | 7 | 7 | 7 |
| Seats to also pay our time ($25/hr) | 12 | 10 | 10 | 9 |

*Assumes a residential-style rental at $575 per event (5 hrs × $100 + $75 fees), which includes the kitchen. All figures are before income tax.*

**Year one (Jan–Dec 2027):**

| | Conservative | Base | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|
| Events | 32 | 37 | 37 |
| Paid guests per event | 8 | 10 | 12 |
| Ticket revenue | $42.4k | **$67.6k** | $85.2k |
| Cash contribution (after all operating costs) | –$2.4k | **$15.7k** | $29.4k |
| Economic profit (after paying ourselves $25/hr) | –$15.2k | **$0.9k** | $14.6k |
| Year-end cash, after $13.3k of startup and studio costs | –$15.7k | **+$2.4k** | +$16.2k |

**The three conclusions that drive this plan:**

1. **The business only works at $175 or more per ticket.** At $145 and 10 guests, we earn about $13/hour. The $145 Founding dinners are a launch tool, not the business.
2. **Seats sold matter more than anything else.** Moving from 8 to 12 paid guests is the difference between losing $15k and making $15k. That makes a minimum seat count for every event non-negotiable.
3. **A dedicated studio is a later decision, not a launch decision.** At $2,300/month all-in, it only matches per-event rentals at 4 events a month. It starts paying off at 5 or more events a month, or once the space brings in other income. In the Conservative case, signing the lease is what turns a small loss into a –$15.7k hole.

## 2. The concept and brand

**Tash Mashup:** *Not just dinner. An evening worth gathering for.*

- **Format:** 10–12 guests, one long communal table, 5–6 courses, about 3 hours, with the hosts at the table.
- **Rotation:** a different theme every dinner, so the brand stands for discovery rather than one cuisine.
- **Setting:** residential feel, with candles, linens, music and our own tableware, which travels with us. It should never feel like a restaurant's private dining room.

**Event series (how the rotation works):**

| Series | What it is | Typical tier |
|---|---|---|
| **The Cultural Mashup** | Two culinary traditions in conversation (e.g., Mumbai × Seoul) | Signature |
| **The Pairing Series** | Food with wine, coffee, tea or zero-proof pairings by a local specialist | Collaboration |
| **The Collaboration Series** | Guest chef, artist, musician or creator co-hosts | Collaboration |
| **The Experience Series** | Travel, music or a season sets the menu and the room | Premium |

**Roles:**

| Area | Owner |
|---|---|
| Menu, culinary concept, kitchen execution | Wife (culinary lead) + supplemental help |
| Venues, partnerships, pricing, finance, ticketing, customer list | You (commercial lead) |
| Brand, creative direction, hosting, content | Shared |

## 3. Customer and market

**Target guest:** San Diegans aged 28–55 who love food and travel, already spend $150–$250 on a special night out, prefer small gatherings, and want an evening worth talking about. Secondary audiences are hospitality professionals, creatives and corporate or social groups booking the whole table.

**Proof of price:** local and comparable supper clubs already sell at these prices: Two Ducks (San Diego) $185, The Blank Table (San Diego) $275, South Fork Supper Club $175, Nocturne (Denver) $195. Some include drinks, tax or gratuity. $175–$200 is mainstream for this category.

**Why Tash Mashup wins:** the rotation plus collaborations give regulars a reason to come back. The intimacy and the hosts' presence are something restaurants can't copy.

## 4. Pricing strategy (flexible and tiered)

| Tier | Price | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Founding** | $145 | First 2–3 dinners only. Sold as limited "founding seats," never called a discount. |
| **Signature** | $175 → $195 | The core product. Raise the price once dinners sell out with a waitlist. |
| **Premium** | $200 | Holidays, premium ingredients, special themes. |
| **Collaboration** | $225+ | Guest talent. The partner fee is built into the model. |

**Rules:**
- **Prepaid only.** Non-refundable within 7 days, but tickets are transferable to someone else.
- **Minimum seats:** Signature/Premium go ahead at **≥10 paid**, Collaboration at ≥9 (the economic break-even). If it isn't met 7 days out, move the date. Book venues with that cancellation window.
- Prices exclude sales tax. Confirm CDTFA treatment of ticketed meals and either add tax at checkout or price tax-inclusive. **No alcohol in the ticket.** Pairings are sold separately by a licensed partner.
- Never sell a regular dinner below $145. To fill the last seats, use "bring a friend" referral credit instead.

## 5. Venue strategy (flexible, phased)

There are three options, all modeled side by side on the `Venue Compare` tab:

| Option | Cost (model) | Feel | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Residential-style rental** (Hillcrest Loft ~$100/hr, The Space SD ~$125/hr) | ~$575 per event | High | **Launch: months 1–6** |
| **B. Restaurant or host-facility residency** on a closed night | greater of $250 or 15% of revenue (~$263) | Medium | Collaborations, and as a permit-friendly path |
| **C. Dedicated Tash Mashup studio** (700–1,000 sq ft flex/live-work unit with kitchen + bath) | $2,300/mo net + ~$9k setup | Highest, and it's ours | **Only after passing the decision gate** |

**Monthly cash contribution, Signature dinner, 10 guests:**

| Events/mo | Rental | Studio | Residency |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | $690 | –$460 | $1,315 |
| 4 | $1,515 | $1,515 | $2,765 |
| 6 | $2,340 | $3,490 | $4,215 |

At 6 events a month, the studio saves about $1,150/month over rentals and pays back its setup in about 8 months.

**Studio decision gate (end of month 6). Sign a lease only if all of these are true:**
1. Three months in a row averaging **≥10 paid seats at ≥$175**, with a waitlist.
2. Realistic demand for **≥5 events a month**, *or* ≥$800/month of other studio income (cooking classes, private bookings, renting to other chefs).
3. The City has confirmed the property's zoning and occupancy, the County has confirmed the food-service setup, and the kitchen needs **no** hood or grease buildout.
4. Short lease (≤2 years) or an exit clause, with total setup under $10k.

The default schedule moves to the studio in July so you can see its effect. If the gate isn't passed, change those months to `Rental` on the `Launch Schedule` tab.

## 7. Partnership structures

These are modeled on the `Partner Deals` tab, using a Signature dinner with 10 guests ($988 contribution before venue or partner costs):

| Partner | Structure | Cost per event | We keep |
|---|---|---|---|
| Restaurant / venue | **Greater of $250 or 15%** (preferred) | $263 | $725 |
| Restaurant / venue | Flat $300 | $300 | $688 |
| Restaurant / venue | 20% revenue share (avoid at high prices) | $350 | $638 |
| Creator | $15 per referred ticket + 1 comp seat | ~$101 | $887 |
| Guest chef | $300 flat, or 30% of contribution | ~$300 | ~$690 |
| Wine shop (licensed) | They sell pairings; we get 10% referral | +$40 income | $1,028 |
| Brand sponsor | $200 cash + $100 in product | +$300 income | $1,288 |

**Non-negotiable in every agreement:** Tash Mashup owns the ticketing, customer list, brand, social handles and photo rights. The agreement must also say in writing who is responsible for food safety, permits, insurance, staff, damage, and cancellation if the seat minimum isn't met.

## 9. Compliance (this decides whether we launch)

Nothing is sold until all of these are confirmed in writing:
- **County DEH:** the correct permit for ticketed dinners (direct-sales catering vs. private-event catering) and approved host-facility status for each venue. "Private supper club" is not an exemption.
- **City of San Diego:** business tax certificate. For any studio, zoning and occupancy verification **before** signing a lease.
- **Food safety:** Food Protection Manager certification (culinary lead) and food handler cards for helpers.
- **Insurance:** general and product liability, with venues named as additional insured.
- **Sales tax:** CDTFA seller's permit and how ticketed meals are taxed.
- **Alcohol:** kept out of our sales entirely. A licensed partner sells pairings.

## 10. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation (and where it's in the model) |
|---|---|
| Seats don't sell | Seat minimum per event; Conservative scenario shows the downside |
| The permit path forces a commissary fee | `Assumptions!B13`: $200–$420/month cuts about $2.4k–$5k from the year |
| Rental costs rise | Negotiate a recurring rate; residency fallback; `Venue Compare` tab |
| Owner burnout (≈590 hrs/yr in the base case) | Standardize prep, reuse proven courses, Collaboration dinners share the work |
| Studio lease becomes a drag | Decision gate; short lease; other studio income in `Assumptions!B25` |
| Food cost creep at premium tiers | Recipe cost sheets; check actuals in `Event Log` against `Event P&L` |

## 11. Year-one scorecard

- ≥30 events completed with an average of **≥10 paid seats**
- Average ticket **≥$175** by month 6
- **≥$25 earned per owner-hour** (`Summary` row 28; base case is $26.50)
- 100+ customers on the email list, and ≥25% of seats from repeat guests or referrals by December
- 5 active partners (venue, residency, pairing, creator, guest chef)
- A clear yes or no on the studio, decided from data rather than enthusiasm
